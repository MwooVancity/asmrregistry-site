## ASMR Registry Content Brief — Scratching ASMR

### 1. SEARCH INTENT

Searchers fall into three clear buckets:

- **Relaxation/sleep seekers** — already know scratching triggers their tingles, want to find quality videos quickly (primary)
- **Curious newcomers** — felt something while someone scratched near them and want to understand what happened
- **Sub-type hunters** — specifically looking for one variety: mic scratching, back scratching, nail scratching on surfaces, or scalp scratching

Dominant intent is **navigational/discovery** — get me to the best scratching ASMR videos, organized by type.

### 2. FEATURED SNIPPET OPPORTUNITY

**Paragraph snippet is most likely to win** for "what is scratching ASMR" or "why does scratching ASMR feel good."

**List snippet opportunity** exists for "types of scratching ASMR" — a bulleted list of mic scratching / back scratching / nail scratching / surface scratching / scalp scratching is not currently owned cleanly by any one page. This is the biggest snippet gap.

Best format: **definition paragraph** (50–60 words) followed immediately by a **5-item bullet list** of sub-types.

### 3. PEOPLE ALSO ASK

1. What is scratching ASMR and why does it feel good?
2. Why does scratching on a microphone create ASMR tingles?
3. Is back scratching ASMR the same as mic scratching ASMR?
4. Why do some people feel ASMR from scratching sounds but others don't?
5. What surfaces or materials create the best scratching ASMR sounds?
6. Does ASMR scratching actually help with sleep and anxiety?
7. Who are the most popular ASMR scratching creators on YouTube?
8. What is the difference between ASMR scratching and ASMR tapping?

### 5. SERP CONTENT TYPE

Top 10 dominated by:
- YouTube video results (4–6 of top 10 slots)
- Listicle/guide pages — "best scratching ASMR videos" roundups
- Trigger explainer pages — Healthline, MasterClass covering ASMR broadly
- ASMR directory/tag pages — theasmr.com has a scratching category page

No single page owns this topic cleanly with a directory-focused format organized by sub-type.

### 6. RECOMMENDED SECTIONS

1. **What scratching ASMR is** — short definition, why the brain responds. 100–150 words max.
2. **The main sub-types** — mic scratching, skin/back scratching, nail scratching on surfaces, scalp scratching, object scratching (wood, glass, plastic, metal)
3. **Why scratching specifically triggers ASMR** — CT-touch nerve pathway angle
4. **What to look for in quality scratching ASMR videos** — mic quality, nail length, surface variety, binaural vs. mono, no talking vs. whispering
5. **Best creators to explore** — named YouTube channels known for scratching content
6. **Scratching + tapping combined** — extremely common pairing; a section on why these two triggers pair well
7. **Who responds to scratching ASMR (and who doesn't)** — scratching is slightly more polarizing than whispering

### 7. CONTENT GAPS

- **No sub-type taxonomy in one place** — every page conflates mic scratching with back scratching
- **No "why scratching specifically"** — all ASMR explainers cover scratching as one bullet in a generic trigger list
- **No creator comparison by sub-type**
- **No pairing guide** — scratching almost always combined with tapping
- **No "not working for me" section**

### 8. WORD COUNT TARGET

**Minimum to compete: 900–1,200 words** for a focused, directory-style page with sub-type taxonomy and embedded video links.
