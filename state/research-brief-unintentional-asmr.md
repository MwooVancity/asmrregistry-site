## UNINTENTIONAL ASMR — Content Brief for asmrregistry.com

### 1. SEARCH INTENT

Searchers split into two groups:

- **Curious newcomers** who stumbled on a video that gave them tingles and didn't know why — searching to name the phenomenon and understand why Bob Ross, a barber video, or a cooking tutorial made them feel relaxed.
- **Active hunters** who already know what unintentional ASMR is and want curated lists of the best videos/channels.

Dominant intent: **informational-discovery hybrid**: explain what it is, then immediately give them examples. Pages that are purely definitional underperform.

### 2. FEATURED SNIPPET OPPORTUNITY

**Format that wins: paragraph snippet** for the definition query, followed by a **list snippet** for "best unintentional ASMR examples." The definition snippet is held by theasmr.com and ineedtosleepnow.com with short 2–3 sentence paragraphs. A tighter definition paragraph (under 60 words, leading with contrast vs. intentional ASMR) plus a structured examples list could claim both.

### 5. SERP CONTENT TYPE

Top 10 split roughly:
- 4–5 listicle/roundup posts
- 2–3 definitional explainer posts
- 1–2 YouTube channel pages
- 1 news/media piece

No structured directory in top 10. asmrregistry.com's video-directory format is differentiated if it indexes real videos by trigger type.

### 6. RECOMMENDED SECTIONS

1. **What is unintentional ASMR** — crisp definition, contrast with intentional
2. **Why it hits differently** — authenticity and lack of self-awareness as trigger amplifiers
3. **Classic examples by category** — TV personalities (Bob Ross, Mr. Rogers), trade/craft videos (barbers, woodworkers), medical/clinical (eye exams), instructional/educational
4. **Best channels and sources** — Pure Unintentional ASMR, Best Unintentional ASMR YouTube channels
5. **Most popular triggers** — soft-spoken voice, task focus, repetitive motion sounds, no music
6. **How to identify unintentional ASMR** — the "creator didn't know" test
7. **Directory: browse unintentional ASMR videos**

### 7. CONTENT GAPS

- **Browsable, filterable video index** — every top result describes the phenomenon but sends users to YouTube with no curation
- **Why unintentional ASMR feels stronger** — real recurring community debate with no good standalone answer
- **Historical context** — pre-YouTube unintentional ASMR (late-night radio, PBS painting shows)
- **Trigger taxonomy specific to unintentional sources**
- **How to find more on YouTube/TikTok** — search operators, playlist curation strategies

### 8. WORD COUNT TARGET

Target **1,400–1,800 words** of prose plus the video directory component.
