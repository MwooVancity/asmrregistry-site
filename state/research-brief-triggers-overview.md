## ASMR TRIGGERS OVERVIEW — CONTENT BRIEF

### 1. SEARCH INTENT

Mixed navigational + informational. Two distinct searcher types:

- **ASMR newcomers** — just heard the term, want to understand what triggers are and whether they can feel them. They want a plain-English explanation with concrete examples, not a science paper.
- **Existing ASMR fans** — know what ASMR is, want a comprehensive list to find new trigger types they haven't tried, or to find videos for a specific trigger.

The page needs to serve both: a crisp definition up top (answer the newcomer in 2 sentences), then a dense, well-organized trigger inventory (serve the fan).

### 2. FEATURED SNIPPET OPPORTUNITY

**Paragraph snippet** currently dominates for "what are asmr triggers." Cleveland Clinic and Healthline both hold snippet-eligible openings with tight, direct definitions. Format that wins:

> "ASMR triggers are specific sights, sounds, or sensations that produce a tingling feeling starting at the scalp and moving down the neck and spine. Common triggers include whispering, tapping, crinkling paper, and slow hand movements."

A **list snippet** opportunity exists for "asmr triggers list" — no single page cleanly owns a 6–8 item bullet list at position zero. That is a gap to exploit. Build a short, scannable trigger list near the top of the page with clean HTML `<ul>` markup.

### 5. SERP CONTENT TYPE

Top 10: long-form listicle guides (1,800–2,800 words) from health/wellness publishers and ASMR-specific sites. Structural pattern: definition → categorized list → science/benefits → FAQ.

### 6. RECOMMENDED SECTIONS

1. What is an ASMR trigger? — 2-sentence snippet-optimized answer above the fold
2. Auditory triggers — whispering, tapping, scratching, crinkling, typing, eating sounds, page turning
3. Visual triggers — hand movements, paint mixing, light patterns, slime, kinetic sand
4. Personal attention triggers — roleplay scenarios, direct address, soft-spoken
5. Why ASMR triggers don't work for everyone — habituation, the ~80% who don't get tingles
6. How to find your triggers — start with whispering/tapping, use headphones, rotate creators
7. ASMR trigger combinations — layered triggers can be more effective than single triggers

### 7. CONTENT GAPS

- Habituation / trigger fatigue — triggers stop working with overuse; underserved topic
- How to find YOUR triggers — newcomer discovery path is absent from competitors
- ASMR trigger combinations — layering whispering + tapping + personal attention
- Video directory angle — linking from trigger categories to actual videos is a native moat no competitor has
