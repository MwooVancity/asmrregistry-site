## Ocean ASMR — Content Brief for asmrregistry.com

### 1. SEARCH INTENT

Searchers fall into three clear buckets:

- **Sleep aid seekers** (largest): people with insomnia, anxiety, or racing thoughts who want ocean sounds to fall asleep faster. Want a reliable, long-format audio/video they can play on repeat.
- **Study/focus users**: need ambient background noise that doesn't distract.
- **Stress/anxiety relief**: daytime decompression, often on a break or during meditation.

Unifying need: a curated shortcut to the best ocean ASMR content without scrolling YouTube for 20 minutes.

### 2. FEATURED SNIPPET OPPORTUNITY

**Paragraph snippet** is the dominant format for "what is ocean ASMR" and "ocean sounds ASMR benefits." The pattern that wins: 40–60 word definition block — define ASMR, explain ocean-specific triggers (rhythmic wave sounds, tidal ebb/flow, pink noise properties), name the outcome (reduced heart rate, lower cortisol, sleep onset).

**List snippet** opportunity for "ocean ASMR types" — 5–6 item list of sound types (crashing waves, rain on beach, binaural tide, storm ambience, underwater) could own a PAA pull.

### 5. SERP CONTENT TYPE

Top 10 dominated by:
- **YouTube videos** (4–6 positions): long-format (1–3 hour) sleep/study videos
- **Article/listicle pages** (2–3 positions): Green Matters article explaining what ocean ASMR is. 600–1,200 words.
- **Streaming/playlist pages** (1–2 positions): Spotify, Apple Music, earth.fm

For a directory page, the article/listicle format is the viable entry point.

### 6. RECOMMENDED SECTIONS

1. **What ocean ASMR is** — concise definition + why ocean sounds specifically trigger the ASMR response (rhythmic predictability, pink noise frequency profile)
2. **Types of ocean ASMR** — crashing waves, gentle tide, rain + ocean combo, stormy seas, underwater/binaural, ocean + fire crackling — each with distinct use case
3. **Best uses by context** — sleep vs. study vs. anxiety relief vs. meditation
4. **The science in plain English** — heart rate reduction, brain relaxation response, pink noise research
5. **Ocean ASMR vs. white noise vs. rain ASMR** — comparison searchers explicitly want
6. **Curated video directory** — organized by type and duration
7. **Tips for getting the most out of ocean ASMR** — headphones vs. speakers, binaural requires headphones, looping, pairing with breathing exercises

### 7. CONTENT GAPS

- **No comparison table**: ocean vs. rain vs. white noise vs. brown noise
- **No binaural explanation**: most articles don't explain what binaural means or why it requires headphones
- **No duration guidance**: how long should a session be?
- **No sub-type taxonomy**: crashing waves vs. gentle tide vs. stormy sea are treated as interchangeable
- **No "doesn't work for me" angle**
- **No device/speaker guidance**: binaural content requires headphones but articles never say this

### 8. WORD COUNT TARGET

Ocean-specific pages currently 500–900 words. **Target for gap-capture: 1,200–1,400 words** — enough to cover the comparison table, binaural explainer, and sub-type taxonomy that no current top-10 result has.
